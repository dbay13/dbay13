# Making money with Scriptorium

Scriptorium is a private diary sealed in wax. People pay for how it feels, so every way of earning sells
more of that feeling: their own seal, rarer wax, a real book, a real stamp. Writing stays free.

## Rules we keep

1. **Writing, sealing and privacy are always free.** Sealing a page is free, and so is locking the whole
   diary. A diary that holds your secrets hostage loses trust, and trust is the product.
2. **One-time purchases before subscriptions.** Journalers keep an app for years and resent paying
   every month for a notebook. A subscription only makes sense later, for something that keeps arriving
   (see "The Guild").
3. **Let people try before they buy.** You can design your own seal and watch it render before paying.
   The purchase only makes it permanent.
4. **Never invent urgency.** Seasonal items really do leave when the season ends, and come back next year.

## Ten ways to earn, from simplest to boldest

### 1. The Patronage: one payment, your own seal
The main earner. Its star feature is **the seal with your initial**: people see their own letter pressed
into wax and it becomes theirs. The prototype lets you type your initial, pick a wax and see the seal
before paying. That moment is the sales pitch.

### 2. The Signet Shop: small, collectible seal designs
Packs of one or two emblems at $0.99–$1.99: *The Royal House* (crown and star), *The Walled Garden*
(rose), *The Wayfarer* (key and moon). They cost little to make, because the renderer draws any emblem in
any wax, so a new pack is a new drawing and a name. Collectors buy them all. Add a pack every month or two.

### 3. Seasonal wax that really leaves
Each season gets one wax that disappears when it ends and returns the next year: **All Hallows'** (black wax and
a crescent moon, until All Souls' Day), **Yule**, **Candlemas**, **Midsummer**. It gives people a reason to
open the app, a reason to post about it, and four sales a year.

### 4. Sealed letters: the growth engine
Write a page for a friend and send it as a link. They see a folded letter with your wax seal, tap to break it,
and read your words on parchment, with a quiet "Written in Scriptorium". Every letter is an ad delivered by
a friend. Free users send one a week; Patrons send as many as they like.
Birthday letters, anniversary letters and "open when you're sad" letters are natural fits.

### 5. Bind thy year: a printed book
At the end of the year, offer a clothbound book of that year's pages, printed with illuminated initials and
a seal on the cover. Sealed pages stay out unless the owner chooses otherwise. Print-on-demand book partners
handle printing and shipping. Physical goods may be sold outside Apple's in-app purchase, so the margin is ours.
December and New Year are the time to sell it.

### 6. A real seal: the app made physical
A brass wax-seal stamp engraved with the same initial and die as the in-app seal, plus three sticks of matching
wax. Seal-stamp makers already engrave custom dies; we send them the design. It also works as a gift
("a seal and a diary for someone who writes letters").

### 7. Guided codices: journaling journeys
Paid sets of questions over 30 days, at $2.99 each:
- *The Pilgrim's Road*: a month of reflection
- *The Gratitude Psalter*: a small thanks each day
- *A Book of Hours for Hard Times*: for grief and heavy seasons, written with care
- *The Campaign Chronicle*: for D&D players, to keep a character's diary between sessions

Journalers already pay for guided journals on paper. These cost us only writing.

### 8. Licensed editions with fantasy authors and game makers
A fantasy author's house sigil as a seal, with questions set in their world, released alongside a new book.
Fans of the book buy the edition, and the author promotes it to their readers. Revenue is shared. One good
partnership can bring more users than months of posting.

### 9. Gifts
Holiday, birthday and graduation gifts: "Give the Patronage" through App Store offer codes, sold in bundles
with the physical seal. Diaries have always been gifts.

### 10. Later, only if people ask: the Guild
A small subscription ($2.99 a month or $19.99 a year) for people who want everything new as it arrives:
every signet, every seasonal wax and every codex. Start it only once there is a steady flow of new things
to give.

## Where the first users come from

- **Short videos.** A seal cracking in two, a candle burning down, an initial being pressed into gold wax.
  Five-second clips for TikTok, Instagram Reels and Shorts. Post one a day for a month before launch.
- **BookTok and the romantasy crowd.** Readers who love fantasy and aesthetics. Send the app to a few
  dozen creators before launch.
- **D&D and tabletop players.** The Campaign Chronicle codex, posted where players gather.
- **Journaling communities.** People who already keep diaries and like beautiful tools.
- **App Store featuring.** Apple tends to feature apps that are well crafted, private and collect no data.
  Apply through the App Store's featuring form a few weeks before launch.
- **Calendar moments.** New Year is the biggest journaling month of the year; Halloween for the seasonal wax;
  December for the printed book and gifts.

## A worked example (not a prediction)

These numbers are made up to show how the pieces add up. Real rates vary a lot.

| | Example rate | Year one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downloads | | 20,000 |
| Become Patron at $7.99 | 5% | 1,000 × $7.99 = $7,990 |
| Buy signets, about $2 each | 3% | 600 × $2 = $1,200 |
| Seasonal wax at $0.99 | 4% | 800 × $0.99 = $792 |
| Candles, about $3 each | 0.5% | 100 × $3 = $300 |
| In-app subtotal | | $10,282 |
| After Apple's 15% (Small Business Program) | | about $8,740 |
| Printed books, about $15 profit each | 0.3% | 60 × $15 = $900 |
| Real seals, about $12 profit each | 0.2% | 40 × $12 = $480 |
| **Total** | | **about $10,100** |

At this scale it is side income. What changes it is reach: one video that travels, one App Store feature or
one author partnership can multiply downloads several times over, and every stream above grows with them.

## Before building more: test the demand

1. Post the seal-cracking and initial-pressing moments from the prototype as short videos.
2. Link to a simple waitlist page.
3. If a few hundred people join in a month, build the Patronage and the Signet Shop first.
   If no one does, change the hook before spending more time.
